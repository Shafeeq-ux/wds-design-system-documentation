Proposed. These principles are drawn from the copy in the WDS 3.0 Figma file. Marketing should confirm them, along with any existing Tekion brand voice guide.

Pattern purpose

Every page on tekion.com should sound like it was written by the same team: people who know dealerships well and want to save dealers time. This page sets the voice (who we are, always the same) and the tone (how we adjust to the moment).

Voice and tone

Four voice principles

Clear

Say the thing. One idea per sentence. Plain words over jargon.

Confident

State what the platform does. No "might", "could help" or "aims to".

Concrete

Show the outcome with numbers, roles and real workflows.

Dealer-first

Write about the dealer's day, not about Tekion's technology.

Writing rules

  • Lead with the benefit, then explain how. "Close deals faster with one connected desk", not "Our desking module integrates with CRM".
  • Use "you" and "your dealership". Use "we" sparingly, and only for Tekion as a company.
  • Prefer short sentences. Fragments are fine for rhythm ("No new vendor. No separate login."), but no more than three in a row.
  • Name roles: service advisor, F&I manager, controller, dealer principal. They help readers find themselves.
  • Spell out acronyms on first use: "dealer management system (DMS)". ARC, T1 and RO can stand alone after that.
  • Back every claim with a number, a customer or a specific capability. Remove superlatives you can't prove.
  • Keep product names exact: Automotive Retail Cloud (ARC), Automotive Enterprise Cloud (AEC), Automotive Partner Cloud (APC), T1, T1 Pro, Tekion One.

Character or word limits

Voice doesn't change the limits. Shorter is almost always better. See Character limits for every slot.

Approved examples

Approved

  • "Your AI doesn't just answer. It gets things done."
  • "Every Department. Every Role. One AI."
  • "Ask in plain English. Zero training."

Not approved

  • "Leveraging cutting-edge synergies to revolutionise retail." (jargon, no outcome)
  • "The best DMS in the world!" (unprovable, exclamation)
  • "Tekion may help improve efficiency." (hedging)

Accessibility and localisation notes

  • Aim for a reading level around grade 8 to 9 for marketing copy. Short sentences help everyone, including people reading in a second language.
  • Avoid idioms and wordplay that don't translate ("hit it out of the park").
  • Don't rely on tone alone to carry meaning. Sarcasm and irony read badly in screen readers and translations.
