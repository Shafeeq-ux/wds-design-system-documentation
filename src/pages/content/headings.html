Pattern purpose

Headings let people scan a page and decide where to read. They also give screen reader users and search engines the page's outline. Each heading has two separate choices: its level (h1 to h4, set by the outline) and its style (Headline 1 to 6, set by the layout).

Voice and tone

Headings state a benefit or a fact in as few words as possible. They can be a short phrase, a statement or a series of fragments ("Ask. Decide. Act."). They're never a teaser that only makes sense after reading the body.

  • Don't skip levels: an h3 must sit under an h2.
  • The eyebrow above a heading ("Automotive Retail Cloud") is a paragraph, not a heading, and shouldn't repeat the heading's words.

Capitalisation

The WDS 3.0 Figma copy uses Title Case for headings, CTAs, navigation and field labels. Body copy, descriptions and messages use sentence case. Overlines are set in capitals by the style, so type them in Title Case.

  • Capitalise the first and last word and every major word.
  • Keep articles, short conjunctions and short prepositions lowercase: a, an, the, and, but, or, for, of, in, on, to, at, by, with.
  • Keep product and acronym casing: "T1", "F&I", "AI-Native", "ARC".

Decision needed. A few Figma headings use sentence case ("They switched. Here's what changed"). Marketing should confirm Title Case as the standard, and those headings should be updated to match.

Punctuation

  • No full stop at the end of a single-phrase heading.
  • Full stops are fine between fragments ("Ask. Decide. Act."). Use the same style for every heading in a section.
  • FAQ questions end with a question mark. Never use exclamation marks.
  • Use an en dash or em dash with no spaces for asides, as in Figma ("T1 Pro—Your Dealership's Conversational AI Interface").

Approved examples

Approved

  • "A Unified Experience Across Every Department"
  • "Enterprise-Ready From One Rooftop to Hundreds"
  • "What happens to my existing data?" (FAQ)

Not approved

  • "Welcome!" (says nothing)
  • "A unified experience across every department" (sentence case for an H2)
  • "The Future Is Here: Discover How Our Revolutionary AI-Native Platform Transforms Everything" (too long, no specifics)

Accessibility and localisation notes

  • Screen reader users often navigate by headings, so every section needs one, even if it's visually small.
  • Don't fake headings with bold paragraphs, or use heading tags just for size.
  • Title Case doesn't exist in most languages. Translated headings should follow each language's own rules.
  • Leave room for 30% longer text in German and French. A heading at its limit in English will overflow.
